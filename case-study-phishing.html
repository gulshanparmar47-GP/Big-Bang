The Problem

Security training usually lists rules nobody remembers under pressure.

Phishing doesn't attack software — it attacks a busy person mid-task who clicks one link. So this module was built to train the instinct, not the rulebook. It drops the learner into the exact moment of temptation — a great deal, a ticking clock, a cursor already drifting toward the link — and lets them feel the decision, including what it costs to get it wrong.

Design Rationale

Training the instinct, not the rulebook

01

Consequence, not correction

The decision point has a real wrong path: click the link, and the learner sees the screen freeze and a file download before recovery is offered. Experiencing the mistake in a safe space teaches more durably than a red "incorrect" flag ever could.

02

Scaffolded difficulty

The six tells are taught on an obvious sneakers scam first, then re-tested on a subtler credential-phishing email disguised as IT. Same skill, harder application — deliberate transfer, not repetition.

03

Reframing the real blocker

People don't report phishing because they feel ashamed. A dedicated screen reframes reporting as protecting the whole company, not confessing a mistake — addressing the attitudinal barrier, not just the knowledge gap.

Working with the tool

Why this was built in Articulate Storyline

This module was built in Articulate Storyline — the corporate e-learning industry's most widely used authoring tool — to demonstrate fluency with branching, states, and triggers. I storyboarded the full narrative, wrote the scenario and its feedback, and built it as a branching interaction with consequence paths and a gated three-question knowledge check, then published to HTML5 and self-hosted it on this site. Alongside my hand-coded and isEazy projects, it shows I can deliver in whatever authoring tool a team already runs on.
